Credit Cards in Switzerland

Switzerland mixes cards, TWINT and still some cash. As a newcomer you usually start with a debit card and apply for a credit card later. This guide explains the difference, the fees to watch and which card setup works for expats.

Who is this for? Expats and newcomers choosing their first Swiss cards — and anyone paying too much in card fees.

Debit vs. credit card

  • Debit card (Debit Mastercard or Visa Debit): comes with your bank account, payments are taken directly from your balance. Accepted in most shops and online.
  • Credit card: payments are collected and billed monthly. Useful for hotels, car rental, deposits and some online purchases where debit cards are not accepted.
  • TWINT: Switzerland's mobile payment app for shops, online and paying friends — linked to your Swiss bank account.

Fees to watch

  • Annual fee: many Swiss credit cards charge one; some cards are free.
  • Foreign currency fees: paying in EUR or other currencies often costs a processing surcharge on top of the exchange rate.
  • Cash withdrawals: withdrawing cash with a credit card is usually expensive.
  • Interest: if you pay the monthly bill in instalments, high interest applies. Paying the full amount each month avoids it.

Compare the price list (fee schedule) of the card before applying — the total cost depends on how you use it.

Using your card abroad

Switzerland is surrounded by euro countries, so foreign currency fees add up quickly. Digital banks and payment apps often charge less for foreign currency than traditional credit cards. When a card terminal abroad offers to charge you in CHF instead of the local currency, choosing the local currency is usually cheaper.

What expats need

  • Start with a Swiss account and debit card — you need a Swiss account for salary, rent and TWINT anyway.
  • Credit check: credit card issuers check your creditworthiness, including the Swiss credit information centre (ZEK). Without a Swiss credit history, approval can take longer.
  • Prepaid cards can bridge the gap until you get a credit card.
  • Keep one card from your home country as a backup for the first months.

For bank accounts and digital banks, see our Finance & Banking guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a debit and a credit card in Switzerland? ▾

A debit card takes payments directly from your account balance. A credit card collects payments and bills them monthly, which is useful for hotels, car rental and deposits.

Can I get a credit card as a newcomer in Switzerland? ▾

Usually yes, but issuers run a credit check, including the ZEK. Without a Swiss credit history approval can take longer; a debit or prepaid card bridges the gap.

Do Swiss credit cards charge foreign transaction fees? ▾

Many do: paying in other currencies often costs a processing surcharge on top of the exchange rate. Check the fee schedule before applying.

Do I need a credit card in Switzerland? ▾

For everyday payments a debit card and TWINT are enough. A credit card helps for hotels, car rental, deposits and some online purchases.

Should I pay in CHF or local currency abroad? ▾

Choosing the local currency is usually cheaper than letting the terminal convert to CHF.
