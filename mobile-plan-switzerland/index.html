📱 Mobile Plans Guide · Switzerland 2026

Prepaid, eSIM or Subscription?

Not sure which mobile plan type makes sense for your situation in Switzerland? This guide breaks down prepaid SIMs, eSIMs and post-paid subscriptions so you can choose with confidence.

Who this is forThis guide helps you choose between prepaid, eSIM and subscription plans — whether you've just arrived, are visiting, or want to switch your existing contract.

1. The three options in a nutshell

OptionCommitmentBest forCost guide
Prepaid SIMNoneArrivals, short stays, maximum flexibilityPay as you go; top-up bundles available
eSIMNone (or contract, depending on plan)Modern phone users arriving in SwitzerlandSimilar to prepaid; varies by provider
Subscription (post-paid)Typically 12–24 monthsLong-term residents wanting best data valueUsually lower monthly cost per GB

All three options give you access to the same Swiss networks (Swisscom, Sunrise, Salt) and their coverage. The main trade-offs are flexibility vs. cost, and how quickly you can get started.

2. Prepaid SIM cards

A prepaid SIM requires no contract and no credit check. You buy a SIM, load credit or a data bundle, and use your phone immediately. When your credit runs out, you top up.

Where to buy

  • SBB Travelshop at major railway stations
  • Provider stores (Swisscom, Sunrise, Salt)
  • Large supermarkets: Migros and Coop carry prepaid SIMs from major networks
  • Electronics stores (Digitec, MediaMarkt)
  • Online, with SIM delivered by post

What you need

Swiss law requires identity verification when registering a SIM card. Bring a valid photo ID — a passport or EU/EEA national identity card is accepted. Some providers allow online registration with an uploaded document.

Pros and cons of prepaid

  • Pro: no contract — stop at any time
  • Pro: no credit check, no Swiss bank account required
  • Pro: available immediately at stations and shops
  • Con: typically costs more per GB than a subscription
  • Con: may need regular top-ups if you use lots of data
💡
Prepaid is ideal for the first weeks in Switzerland. Once you have an address and a Swiss bank account, you can consider switching to a subscription for better value.

3. eSIM options

An eSIM is a digital SIM that you activate by scanning a QR code — no physical card needed. eSIM plans are available in both prepaid and subscription form from Swiss providers.

For a full comparison of eSIM options — including travel eSIMs for immediate arrival connectivity, device compatibility, and dual SIM setups — see our dedicated eSIM in Switzerland guide.

Key points for mobile plan comparisons

  • eSIM prepaid plans work like physical prepaid SIMs but are downloaded digitally.
  • An eSIM subscription is the same as a physical SIM subscription — just activated differently.
  • Your phone must be eSIM-compatible and ideally unlocked. Check your device specs first.
  • Travel eSIMs from international providers are data-only and are a good bridge solution for the first days.

4. Post-paid subscriptions

A post-paid (monthly subscription) plan is the standard long-term choice for Swiss residents. You sign a contract, use your phone, and pay at the end of the month.

Main providers and their MVNOs

  • Swisscom — largest network, widest rural coverage. MVNO: Wingo (Swisscom network, typically lower prices).
  • Sunrise — excellent coverage, merged with UPC. MVNO: Yallo (Sunrise network).
  • Salt — competitive pricing, good urban coverage. MVNO: Spusu (Salt network).

MVNOs typically offer lower monthly prices than the main networks, while using the same physical infrastructure. The trade-off may be in customer service responsiveness or certain premium features.

What you typically need for a subscription

  • Swiss residential address
  • Valid photo ID
  • Swiss bank account (for SEPA direct debit) — some providers accept credit cards
  • A positive credit check (providers run a check — a recent arrival with no Swiss credit history may face restrictions)

EU roaming on Swiss plans

Switzerland is not in the EU. EU roaming on your Swiss plan — i.e. how your plan works when you travel to EU countries — depends on the specific plan. Some plans include EU roaming as standard; others charge per day or per MB. Always check the roaming terms before travelling to EU countries. This is not the same as "roam like at home" rules that apply within the EU.

Contract length and cancellation

Most Swiss mobile subscriptions run for 12 or 24 months. Check the notice period (Kündigungsfrist) in your contract — typically 1–3 months. If you leave Switzerland before the contract ends, contact your provider; some offer early termination options.

5. Which is best for you?

Decision guide
  • →
    Short-term visitor (less than 1 month)?
    A prepaid SIM or travel eSIM is the simplest option. No commitment required.
  • →
    Just arrived, uncertain about address or length of stay?
    Prepaid gives you maximum flexibility. Upgrade to a subscription once you're settled.
  • →
    Working in Switzerland long-term?
    A subscription plan typically offers the best data-to-price ratio. Compare plans from both main networks and MVNOs.
  • →
    Travel frequently to EU countries?
    Check whether EU roaming is included in the plan you're considering. Not all Swiss plans include EU roaming as standard.
  • →
    Family with multiple users?
    Some providers offer family plans or multi-line discounts. Compare both individual and family/multi-SIM options before deciding.
  • →
    Want connectivity from the moment you land?
    Activate a travel eSIM before departure for data from day one, then get a local SIM or eSIM once you're set up.

6. Tips for switching providers

If you already have a Swiss mobile plan and want to switch, here are the key points:

  • Check your notice period: most contracts require 1–3 months' written notice before the end of the contract or billing period. Missing this can lock you in for another full term.
  • Number portability: you can keep your Swiss phone number when switching providers. This is called Rufnummernmitnahme (number portability). Your new provider usually handles the process — give them your current number and contract details.
  • Check for activation fees: some new plans charge a one-time activation or SIM card fee.
  • Overlap billing: if your old plan runs until the end of the month and your new plan starts on day one, you may pay briefly for both. Align start dates if possible.
  • eSIM transfer: if switching to an eSIM plan, your old physical SIM will be deactivated once the eSIM is active — make sure you have the new plan confirmed and ready to activate.
