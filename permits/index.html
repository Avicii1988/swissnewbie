Residence Permits in Switzerland — L, B, C & G Explained

Moving to Switzerland means getting the right permit. The type depends on your nationality (EU/EFTA or non-EU), your employment contract length, and how long you plan to stay. Here is what you need to know.

Lena Keller
Swiss Relocation Specialist
Sources: sem.admin.ch · ch.ch · AIG · FZA

The four main permit types

L

L Permit — Short-term residence

For stays up to 12 months, typically tied to a fixed-term contract. EU/EFTA nationals with a contract of 3–12 months receive an L permit. Non-EU nationals with a limited assignment may also receive it. Not renewable in the same form — requires a new contract or upgrade to B.

B

B Permit — Renewable residence

The standard permit for people moving to Switzerland long-term. EU/EFTA nationals with a contract of 12 months or more receive a B permit valid for 5 years. Non-EU nationals receive a 1-year B permit, subject to annual renewal and cantonal quotas. Full B permit guide →

C

C Permit — Settlement (unlimited)

An unlimited residence permit. EU/EFTA nationals can apply after 5 continuous years; most non-EU nationals after 10 years (some nationalities after 5 years under bilateral agreements). Requires no annual renewal and allows free job and canton changes.

G

G Permit — Cross-border commuter

For people who work in Switzerland but live abroad (in a bordering country). You must return to your foreign residence at least once a week. EU/EFTA commuters in a bordering zone: 5-year G permit. Non-EU commuters: governed by specific bilateral treaties.

EU/EFTA nationals vs non-EU nationals

🇪🇺

EU/EFTA nationals

Rights governed by the Agreement on Free Movement of Persons (FZA/AFMP). B permit for 5 years with a 12-month+ contract. No quota. Self-employed: B permit on proof of activity. Register at the Gemeinde within 14 days of arrival.

🌍

Non-EU/EFTA nationals

Governed by the Foreign Nationals and Integration Act (AIG). Subject to national quotas (Inländervorrang — Swiss and EU candidates considered first). Annual B permit, renewed yearly by the cantonal migration authority. Employer sponsorship typically required.

Your first steps after arriving

  1. Register within 14 days — visit your local Einwohnerkontrolle (residents' registration office) with your passport and rental contract. Arrival registration guide →
  2. Enrol in health insurance within 3 months — coverage is backdated to arrival. Missing this window means the canton assigns you an insurer. Health insurance guide →
  3. Open a Swiss bank account — needed for salary payments and most service contracts. Finance guide →
  4. Set up your Swiss mobile plan — local SIM required by most employers and services. Mobile guide →

⏰ Don't miss the health insurance deadline

Swiss law requires KVG/LAMal health insurance from day one of residence. You have 3 months to choose a provider — after that, the canton assigns one for you, often at a higher premium. Start comparing now.

Frequently asked questions

Do EU/EFTA citizens need a residence permit in Switzerland? ▾

Yes. EU/EFTA citizens exercising a right under the AFMP/FZA must register and obtain a permit. For employment of 12 months or more they receive a B permit (5 years); for shorter contracts an L permit. Registration at the Gemeinde must happen within 14 days of arrival.

What is the difference between a B and C permit? ▾

The B permit is a renewable residence permit (5 years for EU/EFTA, 1 year for non-EU). The C permit is an unlimited settlement permit: EU/EFTA can apply after 5 continuous years, non-EU nationals generally after 10 years.

How quickly must I register after arriving? ▾

You must register at your local residents' registration office (Einwohnerkontrolle) within 14 days of taking up residence. Bring your passport, a valid rental or sub-rental contract, and your employment contract. The permit application is initiated there or forwarded to the cantonal migration authority.

When must I take out Swiss health insurance? ▾

Everyone living in Switzerland must hold KVG/LAMal basic health insurance. You have 3 months from taking up residence to enrol; coverage is backdated to your arrival date. If you miss the deadline the canton will assign you an insurer — often at a higher premium.
