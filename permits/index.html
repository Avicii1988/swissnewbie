Swiss Residence Permits: L, B, C and G

Who needs which Swiss permit, how to get it, and what comes next — a practical overview for newcomers from both EU/EFTA and non-EU countries.

Who this is for Anyone planning to live or work in Switzerland who needs to understand which residence permit applies to them.
Lena Keller
Swiss Relocation Specialist · SwissNewbie

1. EU/EFTA citizens

Switzerland has bilateral agreements with the EU (the Agreement on the Free Movement of Persons, AFMP) which means EU/EFTA citizens can move to Switzerland freely for employment, self-employment, or job-seeking. There is no visa requirement and no quota for EU/EFTA nationals.

The process is straightforward:

  1. Arrive in Switzerland
  2. Register at your local Gemeinde (commune) within 14 days of taking up residence
  3. Bring your passport or national ID, employment contract (or proof of means), and rental contract
  4. The Gemeinde issues your permit automatically based on your purpose of stay

EU/EFTA citizens with a contract of 12 months or more receive a B permit (5 years). Those with contracts under 12 months receive an L permit. Self-employed EU/EFTA citizens who can demonstrate economic independence also qualify for the B permit.

Source: sem.admin.ch — Free Movement of Persons

2. Non-EU/EFTA citizens

The rules are stricter for citizens of countries outside the EU/EFTA. Switzerland operates a quota-based system for non-EU/EFTA workers under the Foreign Nationals and Integration Act (AIG).

Key points:

  • You generally need a job offer from a Swiss employer before entering Switzerland
  • Your employer must demonstrate there is no suitable Swiss or EU/EFTA candidate available (Inländervorrang / priority for domestic workers)
  • Annual quotas apply; not all applications are approved
  • Permits are issued by the cantonal migration authority and confirmed by the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM)
  • You can apply for your permit once it is approved — processing times vary by canton

The initial B permit for non-EU/EFTA citizens is typically valid for 1 year and is renewable if the qualifying conditions continue to be met.

Source: sem.admin.ch — Third-country nationals

3. The permit types: L, B, C and G

L — Short-stay permit (Kurzaufenthaltsbewilligung)

For stays up to 12 months. Issued to EU/EFTA citizens with fixed-term contracts under one year, and to non-EU/EFTA workers in specific seasonal or short-term categories. L permits are tied to a specific employer and purpose; extensions are possible but time-limited.

B — Residence permit (Aufenthaltsbewilligung)

The standard permit for newcomers who intend to stay longer than 12 months. EU/EFTA citizens receive a 5-year B permit; non-EU/EFTA citizens typically receive a 1-year B permit renewable annually. This is the most common permit for expats arriving for work. See the full B permit guide →

C — Settlement permit (Niederlassungsbewilligung)

An open-ended permit with no expiry, granted after sufficient years of continuous residence (generally 5 years for EU/EFTA citizens, 10 years for non-EU/EFTA). Holders of a C permit are not subject to the labour market conditions that apply to B permit holders. It does not replace citizenship — naturalisation is a separate process.

G — Cross-border commuter permit (Grenzgängerbewilligung)

For people who live in a neighbouring country (Germany, France, Italy, Austria, Liechtenstein) and work in Switzerland, returning to their country of residence at least once a week. Available to EU/EFTA nationals. Non-EU/EFTA nationals from eligible countries may also qualify under specific conditions. The G permit does not authorise residence in Switzerland.

5. Next steps after your permit

Once your permit is in hand, three tasks are time-sensitive:

  • Health insurance: Mandatory within 90 days of arrival. Coverage is backdated to your arrival date, but you must choose a provider yourself — if you don't, your canton assigns one. Choose your health insurance →
  • Bank account: You need a Swiss IBAN for salary payments. Digital expat-friendly banks make opening quick. Finance & Banking guide →
  • Mobile & internet: A Swiss SIM is needed almost immediately for two-factor authentication at banks. Mobile & Internet guide →
