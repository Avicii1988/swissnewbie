📋 Arrival registration guide

Registering Your Arrival in Switzerland — Anmeldung & No Digital Card

There is no digital arrival card for Switzerland. To become a legal resident, you register in person at your commune (Gemeinde) within 14 days. Here is exactly what to do and where the ETIAS travel authorisation stands.

📋

No digital arrival card — as of September 2026

Switzerland has no digital arrival registration or online pre-registration system for incoming residents. Registration happens in person at the local residents' registration office within 14 days of taking up residence. Source: ch.ch.

Swiss Relocation Specialist
Sources: ch.ch · travel-europe.europa.eu/etias · cantonal migration authorities

The Anmeldung: registering at your commune

1

Find your Einwohnerkontrolle

Each commune (Gemeinde) has a residents' registration office — called Einwohnerkontrolle (German), contrôle des habitants (French), or controllo degli abitanti (Italian). Find yours on the commune website or via ch.ch. Many require an appointment.

2

Register within 14 days

Swiss law requires registration within 14 days of taking up residence. This applies to all newcomers — EU/EFTA and non-EU. Registering later can delay your permit and health insurance enrolment.

3

Bring the right documents

Typically: valid passport or national ID, rental contract or landlord confirmation, and employment contract. Some communes also request a birth certificate, marriage certificate, or photos. Check your specific commune's requirements.

4

Permit application is initiated

The Einwohnerkontrolle registers you as a resident and forwards your permit application to the cantonal migration authority (Migrationsamt). For EU/EFTA nationals the permit is usually issued directly; non-EU nationals receive a permit document to collect later.

ETIAS: Switzerland's position

ETIAS (European Travel Information and Authorisation System) is a pre-travel authorisation for visa-exempt third-country nationals who want to visit the Schengen Area. Switzerland is a Schengen member state and will participate in ETIAS.

Status as of September 2026: ETIAS is not yet operational. The EU Commission has not confirmed a launch date. When it does launch, citizens of visa-exempt countries (e.g. UK, USA, Australia) will need to apply before visiting Schengen countries — including Switzerland — for short stays. Residents with a valid Swiss residence permit are exempt from ETIAS. Source: travel-europe.europa.eu/etias.

⏰ Register, then get health insurance

After registering at the Gemeinde, your next deadline is health insurance: Swiss KVG/LAMal must be taken out within 3 months of residence. Coverage is backdated — but if you miss the window, the canton assigns you a provider, often at a higher premium.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a digital arrival card for Switzerland? ▾

No. As of September 2026, Switzerland has no digital arrival card or online pre-registration system for new residents. You must register in person at your local Einwohnerkontrolle within 14 days of taking up residence. Source: ch.ch.

What is the Anmeldung and when must I do it? ▾

Anmeldung is the mandatory registration at your local commune. You must register within 14 days of moving in. Bring your passport, rental contract, and employment contract. The registration initiates your permit process. Source: ch.ch.

What is ETIAS and does it apply to Switzerland? ▾

ETIAS is a pre-travel authorisation for visa-exempt third-country nationals entering the Schengen Area. Switzerland is a Schengen member and will participate. As of September 2026, ETIAS is not yet operational. The EU Commission has not announced a confirmed launch date. Residents with a valid Swiss permit are exempt. Source: travel-europe.europa.eu/etias.

What documents do I need for the Anmeldung? ▾

Typically: a valid passport or national identity card, a rental contract or confirmation from your landlord, and (for employed persons) an employment contract. Some communes ask for additional documents such as a birth certificate or marriage certificate. Check your specific commune's website for exact requirements.
