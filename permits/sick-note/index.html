Sick Notes in Switzerland — When Is a Doctor's Certificate Required?

Swiss law does not fix a mandatory day for presenting a sick note. Your employment contract governs when one is required — in practice, many contracts specify day 3. Here is what the law says, what to expect, and what your employer can and cannot do.

Lena Keller
Swiss Relocation Specialist
Sources: fedlex.admin.ch · ch.ch · Art. 324a OR

What Swiss law says

Art. 324a OR (Code of Obligations)

No statutory day for the sick note

Article 324a of the Swiss Code of Obligations (Obligationenrecht / OR) governs continued salary payment during illness. It does not specify from which day of absence an employee must submit a medical certificate (Arztzeugnis). The employer can request proof of incapacity at any time — including from day 1. What your contract says takes precedence, subject to a minimum level of protection that cannot be contractually waived. Source: fedlex.admin.ch, SR 220.

In practice: Many employment contracts in Switzerland — and many collective labour agreements (GAV / CCT) — specify that a medical certificate is required from the third day of illness ("ab dem dritten Krankheitstag"). This is a contractual standard, not a legal minimum. Your contract may require one earlier or later. Always check your employment contract and any applicable GAV.

Continued salary during illness

Under Art. 324a OR, the employer must continue paying salary during illness for a minimum period that scales with years of service. The most commonly referenced scale is the Bern Scale (Berner Skala):

Year 1

At least 3 weeks of continued salary

Year 2

At least 2 months

Years 3–4

At least 3 months

Years 5–9

At least 4 months

Years 10+

At least 5 months

Most employers hold daily sickness benefit insurance (Krankentaggeldversicherung / assurance indemnité journalière maladie) that extends coverage to 730 days at 80% of salary after a defined waiting period. Check your employment contract or staff handbook.

Termination protection during illness

Art. 336c OR prohibits dismissal during certain blocking periods, including periods of illness-related incapacity for work. The blocking period is:

  • 30 days in the first year of service
  • 90 days in years 2–5
  • 180 days from year 6 onwards

A notice of dismissal served during a blocking period is null and void. The period is suspended during the illness and resumes after recovery. Source: fedlex.admin.ch, Art. 336c OR.

Frequently asked questions

From which day do I need a sick note in Switzerland? ▾

Swiss law (Art. 324a OR) does not specify a mandatory day. The employer can request a medical certificate at any time. In practice, many employment contracts specify that a doctor's certificate is required from the third day of illness. Always check your own contract and any applicable collective labour agreement (GAV). Source: ch.ch, fedlex.admin.ch.

Does my employer have to pay me when I'm sick? ▾

Yes. Under Art. 324a OR, your employer must continue paying salary during illness for a minimum period based on years of service (e.g. at least 3 weeks in your first year). Most employers additionally hold daily sickness benefit insurance that extends this to 730 days at 80% of salary.

Can my employer require a sick note from day 1? ▾

Yes. Swiss law does not prohibit requiring a certificate from day 1. If your contract or company rules specify day 1, you must provide it regardless of illness length. This is a contractual matter — employers and employees can agree on the threshold.

What happens if I'm sick for more than 30 days? ▾

Extended illness is typically covered by the employer's Krankentaggeldversicherung (daily sickness benefit insurance), which pays 80% of salary for up to 730 days after a waiting period. During illness, Art. 336c OR also protects you from dismissal for defined blocking periods (30–180 days depending on years of service).
