📋 B Permit Guide · Updated September 2026

B Permit Switzerland: Conditions, Validity, Renewal

The B permit (Aufenthaltsbewilligung) is Switzerland's standard residence permit for newcomers. EU/EFTA citizens get a 5-year permit on registration; non-EU/EFTA citizens receive a 1-year renewable permit through the employer-quota system.

1. EU/EFTA citizens: B permit

EU/EFTA citizens are entitled to the B permit under the Agreement on the Free Movement of Persons (AFMP) between Switzerland and the EU. The permit is issued automatically when you register at your Gemeinde (commune).

Who qualifies:

  • EU/EFTA employees with an employment contract of 12 months or more
  • EU/EFTA self-employed persons who can demonstrate economic independence
  • EU/EFTA citizens who are financially self-sufficient (e.g. retirees or financially independent persons with comprehensive health insurance)
  • EU/EFTA students enrolled at a Swiss educational institution (with health insurance and proof of means)

How to get it: Register at your Gemeinde within 14 days of arrival. Bring your passport or national ID, employment contract, and rental contract or proof of address. The permit is issued there and then or sent by post within a short time.

Source: sem.admin.ch — EU/EFTA residence permits

2. Non-EU/EFTA citizens: B permit

For citizens of countries outside the EU/EFTA, the B permit is subject to Switzerland's quota system under the Foreign Nationals and Integration Act (AIG / LEI).

Conditions:

  • You must have a concrete job offer from a Swiss employer
  • The employer must demonstrate there is no suitable Swiss or EU/EFTA candidate (Inländervorrang — priority for domestic workers)
  • Annual quotas limit the number of permits; demand in some occupations and cantons can mean long waits or unsuccessful applications
  • The permit requires approval from the cantonal migration authority and, for certain categories, confirmation by the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM)
  • Highly qualified workers (managers, specialists, university graduates) are the primary beneficiaries of third-country B permits

How to get it: Your employer initiates the application with the cantonal migration authority before you enter Switzerland. Once approved, you collect the permit document at the Gemeinde after your arrival and Anmeldung.

Source: sem.admin.ch — B permit for third-country nationals

3. Validity and renewal

OriginInitial validityRenewal
EU/EFTA citizens5 yearsRenewed for 5 years if qualifying conditions still met
Non-EU/EFTA citizens1 year (typical)Annual renewal while employment/qualifying basis continues

Renewal process: Contact your cantonal migration authority before the permit expires — ideally 3 months in advance. EU/EFTA citizens typically renew at the Gemeinde. Non-EU/EFTA citizens apply through the cantonal migration office. You will need: current permit, valid passport, confirmation of continued employment (or other qualifying basis), and proof of address.

A permit that lapses because you did not renew in time can create complications — renew early.

4. Path to the C permit

The C permit (Niederlassungsbewilligung / settlement permit) is open-ended and not subject to labour market conditions. It is available after a period of continuous legal residence:

  • EU/EFTA citizens: eligible after 5 years of uninterrupted B permit residence, subject to integration requirements (including language skills)
  • Non-EU/EFTA citizens: generally eligible after 10 years of uninterrupted legal residence; integration requirements apply

The C permit does not expire and is not tied to employment. It is not the same as Swiss citizenship — naturalisation is a separate, longer process.

Source: sem.admin.ch — C permit (settlement)
