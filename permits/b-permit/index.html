🪪 B Permit guide

B Permit Switzerland — Validity, Renewal & Path to C

The B permit is Switzerland's standard long-term residence permit. EU/EFTA nationals receive a 5-year permit; non-EU nationals receive a 1-year permit renewed annually. Here is what governs each, what renewal requires, and when you can upgrade to a C permit.

Lena Keller
Swiss Relocation Specialist
Sources: sem.admin.ch · AIG (SR 142.20) · FZA (SR 0.142.112.681)

EU/EFTA vs non-EU nationals

🇪🇺 EU/EFTA nationals

  • Legal basis: Agreement on Free Movement of Persons (FZA)
  • Contract of 12 months or more → B permit for 5 years
  • Self-employed: B permit on proof of economic activity
  • No employer quota; no Inländervorrang
  • Free to change jobs, employers, and cantons
  • Family members: immediate family also receive B permit

🌍 Non-EU/EFTA nationals

  • Legal basis: Foreign Nationals and Integration Act (AIG)
  • Annual B permit, renewed yearly by cantonal migration authority
  • Subject to national quotas (Kontingente)
  • Inländervorrang: Swiss, then EU/EFTA candidates considered first
  • Job/canton changes require notification or new permit
  • Family reunification: income and housing requirements apply

Renewing a non-EU B permit

The cantonal migration authority typically contacts you before expiry. In general, renewal is approved if you:

  • Are still employed by a Swiss employer (or can demonstrate continued economic activity)
  • Have no criminal record entries that trigger a review
  • Have not been dependent on social assistance for extended periods
  • Meet the integration criteria (language level, civic knowledge) if required by your canton
Note: renewal is not automatic. Apply or confirm with your cantonal migration authority at least 2–3 months before your current permit expires to avoid gaps in your legal residence status.

Path to the C permit (settlement permit)

Year 1–5

B permit phase

Hold the B permit, renew as required. Build uninterrupted legal residence record.

Year 5

EU/EFTA: eligible for C permit

Apply to the cantonal migration authority. Requires 5 continuous years of legal residence, no significant criminal record, and evidence of integration. Source: sem.admin.ch/FZA Art. 4.

Year 10

Non-EU: eligible for C permit (general rule)

Most non-EU nationals must hold 10 continuous years of B permit. Some nationalities (USA, Canada, and others under bilateral agreements) qualify after 5 years. Requirements include language proficiency, civic knowledge, financial independence, and integration. Source: sem.admin.ch/AIG Art. 34.

⏰ Health insurance: a permit requirement

Swiss law requires KVG/LAMal health insurance for all residents — including B permit holders — from day one. You have 3 months from taking up residence to enrol; coverage is backdated. Choosing the right insurer and model can save CHF 500–2,000 per year.

Frequently asked questions

How long is a B permit valid in Switzerland? ▾

For EU/EFTA nationals with an employment contract of 12 months or more, the B permit is valid for 5 years. For non-EU nationals, the B permit is issued for 1 year and must be renewed annually by the cantonal migration authority. Source: sem.admin.ch.

Can I change jobs or employers with a B permit? ▾

EU/EFTA B permit holders can change jobs and employers freely. Non-EU permit holders in most cantons must notify the cantonal migration authority; changing to a significantly different occupation or a different canton may require a new permit application, subject to Inländervorrang.

When can I apply for a C permit? ▾

EU/EFTA nationals can apply after 5 continuous years of legal residence in Switzerland. Most non-EU nationals must wait 10 years. Some nationalities (e.g. USA, Canada) qualify after 5 years under bilateral agreements. Source: sem.admin.ch.

Does a B permit cover family members? ▾

EU/EFTA B permit holders can bring immediate family (spouse, dependent children under 21) who also receive a B permit under FZA. Non-EU permit holders may apply for family reunification subject to income thresholds, suitable housing, and cantonal approval. Processing times vary significantly.
