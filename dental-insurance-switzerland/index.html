Dental Insurance in Switzerland

Swiss basic health insurance does not pay for routine dental care. Check-ups, hygiene appointments, fillings and braces are usually paid out of your own pocket — unless you have supplementary dental insurance. Here is what is covered, when an extra plan makes sense and what to watch out for.

What basic insurance does not cover

Mandatory basic health insurance (KVG/LaMal) covers dental treatment only in exceptional cases, for example when it is caused by a severe disease of the masticatory system or by another serious illness. Dental damage from an accident is covered by accident insurance.

Not covered are the costs most people actually have:

  • Regular check-ups and dental hygiene appointments
  • Fillings, root canal treatment and crowns caused by tooth decay
  • Orthodontics (braces), for children and adults
  • Cosmetic treatments such as whitening

How supplementary dental insurance works

Dental cover is a supplementary insurance under private law. That has three consequences:

  • Insurers can refuse you or exclude existing problems. Many ask for a health questionnaire or a dental check before accepting you.
  • Benefits are limited: plans usually reimburse a percentage of the costs up to an annual maximum. Compare both values, not just the premium.
  • You can choose freely: supplementary insurance does not have to be with the same insurer as your basic insurance.

When dental insurance is worth it

Whether a plan pays off depends on your situation. It is often worth considering:

  • For children: orthodontic treatment is common and expensive. Plans for children are typically easier to get when they are taken out early, before any treatment need is known.
  • If you expect treatment: but note that known problems are usually excluded.

If your teeth are healthy and you only need check-ups, putting the premium aside each month can be the simpler option. Compare the annual premium with the maximum benefit you could realistically use.

Waiting periods and exclusions

Many dental plans have a waiting period after signing during which certain treatments are not yet covered, and some reimburse more only after several years of membership. Treatment that was already planned or needed before you signed is usually excluded. Check the general terms (AVB/CGA) for:

  • the length of any waiting period
  • the reimbursement rate and annual maximum
  • exclusions for existing conditions and orthodontics
  • the notice period if you want to leave the plan

Keeping dental costs under control

  • Ask for a written cost estimate before larger treatments and compare a second offer if needed.
  • Regular check-ups and hygiene appointments help avoid expensive treatment later.
  • Many cantons and municipalities run school dental services with check-ups for children — ask your municipality.

Frequently asked questions

Does Swiss basic health insurance cover dental care? ▾

Only in exceptional cases, such as treatment caused by a severe disease. Check-ups, hygiene, fillings and braces are not covered by basic insurance.

Is dental insurance mandatory in Switzerland? ▾

No. Dental cover is an optional supplementary insurance under private law.

Can an insurer refuse my dental insurance application? ▾

Yes. Supplementary insurers can refuse applicants or exclude existing problems, and many ask for a questionnaire or a dental check first.

Is dental insurance worth it for children? ▾

It is often worth considering because orthodontic treatment is common and expensive. Taking out a plan early, before any treatment need is known, usually makes acceptance easier.

Does dental insurance have a waiting period? ▾

Many plans do, and treatment that was already needed before signing is usually excluded. The details are in the policy terms.
