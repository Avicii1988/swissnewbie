eSIM in Switzerland

Stay connected from the moment you land. This guide covers travel eSIMs, Swiss prepaid eSIMs, subscriptions, dual SIM setups, and how to pick the right option for your situation.

Who this is forThis guide is for people planning to move to Switzerland, recent arrivals needing immediate connectivity, cross-border workers, and anyone comparing eSIM vs physical SIM options.

1. What is an eSIM and which phones support it?

An eSIM (embedded SIM) is a digital SIM card built directly into your device. Instead of inserting a physical SIM card, you activate a mobile plan by scanning a QR code or entering an activation code provided by your carrier. The profile is downloaded over the internet and stored in the phone's hardware.

Key differences from a physical SIM

  • No physical card: nothing to swap out or lose.
  • Multiple profiles: many devices can store several eSIM profiles and switch between them (though typically only one is active at a time, unless your device supports dual active eSIM).
  • Instant activation: you can activate a plan minutes after purchase, without waiting for a delivery.

Device compatibility

eSIM support has become widespread, but compatibility still varies by device and carrier. As a general guide:

  • iPhone: supported from iPhone XS (2018) onwards. iPhone 14 and later (US models) are eSIM-only with no physical SIM tray.
  • Android: many flagship and mid-range Android phones support eSIM, including models from Samsung, Google Pixel, and others. Check your specific model's specifications.
  • Important: some phones are carrier-locked and may not support eSIM with other carriers. Check with your device manufacturer or carrier before buying an eSIM plan.
ℹ️
Always verify your phone's eSIM compatibility before purchasing a plan. Manufacturer websites and your phone's Settings → Mobile Data menu are reliable ways to check.

2. Before you arrive: travel eSIM options

A travel eSIM lets you buy and activate data connectivity before you even board your flight — meaning you land in Switzerland with internet access ready to go. Several international providers sell travel eSIMs that cover Switzerland.

Why you may need a separate plan for Switzerland

Switzerland is not a member of the European Union. This means EU roaming regulations do not apply. If you currently have a European mobile plan with "roam like at home" EU coverage, Switzerland is almost certainly not included. Check your existing plan's Switzerland roaming rates explicitly — they can be very high.

What travel eSIMs typically offer

  • Data only: most travel eSIMs provide a data-only connection. You will not have a Swiss phone number for calls or SMS.
  • Prepaid data packages: you purchase a set amount of data (e.g. 3 GB, 10 GB) for a set period.
  • Multiple country coverage: many travel eSIM plans cover a region or multiple countries, making them useful if you'll be travelling through several countries before arriving.

Pros and cons of travel eSIMs

For arrivals, a travel eSIM is an excellent bridge for the first few days while you sort out a local plan. Compare options from international eSIM providers — check their coverage maps to confirm Switzerland is included before purchasing.

3. After arrival: Swiss eSIM and prepaid options

Once you're in Switzerland, you have a range of options from the three main Swiss networks and their MVNOs (mobile virtual network operators).

Swiss prepaid SIM / eSIM

The quickest way to get a Swiss number. Prepaid SIMs and eSIMs require no contract, no bank account, and are available at:

  • Railway station shops (SBB/CFF Travelshop) — available at major stations across Switzerland
  • Provider stores (Swisscom, Sunrise, Salt)
  • Large supermarkets (Migros, Coop) and electronics stores
  • Online, with activation by post or digitally

Note: Swiss law requires identity verification for SIM registration. You will need a valid photo ID (passport or national ID card).

Swiss prepaid plans from main providers

  • Swisscom (Natel Easy prepaid): Swisscom has the widest network coverage in Switzerland. Their prepaid range includes data and call bundles. Check current pricing directly on swisscom.ch.
  • Sunrise: Switzerland's second-largest operator, merged with UPC. Prepaid options available via sunrise.ch.
  • Salt: solid coverage in urban areas and along main transport routes. Prepaid options at salt.ch.

Prices and data bundles change regularly. Always check the provider's current website for up-to-date plans before purchasing.

Post-paid subscriptions

Once you have a Swiss address and ideally a Swiss bank account, a post-paid monthly subscription typically offers better value per GB than prepaid. See our dedicated Mobile Plans guide for a full comparison.

Switzerland is not in the EU. EU roaming tariffs from your Swiss plan for travel to EU countries are a separate line item. Some subscriptions include EU roaming; others charge extra. Always confirm before travelling to EU countries.

5. Dual SIM setup

Many modern smartphones support two active connections simultaneously — one physical SIM and one eSIM, or (on some newer devices) two eSIMs. This is particularly useful for expats and cross-border workers.

Common dual SIM use cases

  • Keep your home country number: retain a foreign SIM for calls and messages from family, while using a Swiss number for local use.
  • Cross-border workers: a Swiss number for work, a home-country SIM for personal use and EU-side calls.
  • Travel eSIM + Swiss number: use a travel eSIM for data on arrival while retaining your existing SIM for calls.

How to set up dual SIM

On most iPhones (iOS 13+), go to Settings → Mobile Data → Add Mobile Plan to add an eSIM. On Android, the process varies by manufacturer — look under Settings → Connections or Settings → Network & Internet. Your device manual or manufacturer support website will have device-specific instructions.

💡
You can label each SIM in your phone settings (e.g. "Switzerland" and "Home") to easily manage which line is used for calls, data, and messages.

6. Network coverage in Switzerland

Switzerland has three national mobile network operators. MVNOs (mobile virtual network operators) run their services on top of these networks:

NetworkCoverage highlightMVNOs using this network
SwisscomBest rural and mountain coverage; 98%+ 5G nationwide population coverageWingo
SunriseExcellent coverage; merged with UPC for combined fixed/mobile offeringYallo
SaltGood coverage in urban areas, along motorways and main rail routesSpusu

Checking coverage before you choose

Coverage maps can look similar at a national level, but differences emerge in valleys, mountain areas, and tunnels. Before committing to a provider:

  • Use each provider's coverage map on their official website to check your home address and workplace.
  • Check coverage along your regular commute route.
  • BAKOM (Federal Office of Communications) publishes independent coverage data at bakom.admin.ch.

If you live or work in a rural or Alpine area, Swisscom typically has the strongest coverage. For urban areas, all three main networks perform well.

7. Decision guide: which option is right for you?

Find your best option
  • →
    Visiting for less than 2 weeks?
    A travel eSIM or roaming from your existing home plan (check Switzerland rates explicitly) is usually the simplest option.
  • →
    Arriving to live, need connectivity from day 1?
    Activate a travel eSIM before you leave for data connectivity from landing. Then get a local prepaid SIM or eSIM in the first few days for a Swiss number.
  • →
    Cross-border commuter (Grenzgänger)?
    Consider a dual SIM setup: a Swiss plan for local calls and data, plus your home country SIM for the other side of the border.
  • →
    Settling long-term (more than 3 months)?
    A local monthly subscription typically offers the best data value once you have a Swiss address. Compare plans from the main networks and MVNOs.
  • →
    Need calls and SMS from day 1?
    A physical SIM card or an eSIM with a voice plan from a Swiss provider is the right choice. Travel eSIMs are typically data-only.
  • →
    Concerned about rural / mountain coverage?
    Swisscom has the widest rural coverage in Switzerland. If you'll be in a remote area frequently, this is worth prioritising.
