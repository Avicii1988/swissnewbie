Priority: Personal liability insurance
This should be your very first insurance after health insurance. Almost every Swiss landlord requires proof of personal liability before signing a lease.
🚨
If you accidentally damage a rental apartment (e.g. spill coffee on a parquet floor, break a bathroom fixture), you're personally liable for the full repair cost — which can easily run CHF 5,000–50,000+. Liability insurance for CHF 100/year covers all of this.
🏠

What it covers

Damage you cause to rented property, to other people's property, or physical injury to third parties — even accidental. Usually CHF 3–10 million coverage.

👨‍👩‍👧

Family policies

A single personal liability policy usually covers your entire household including children and pets. Check the policy details before buying individual policies per person.

⚡

How fast you need it

Get it before — or at the very latest on the day of — signing your rental contract. Many insurers can activate same-day online.

🔄

Bundle & save

Many insurers offer liability + household contents as a bundle for CHF 150–250/year — much cheaper than separate policies.

Frequently asked questions
Do I really need personal liability insurance? ▾

In practice, yes — nearly every Swiss landlord requires it as a condition of renting. Even if you owned property, damages you cause to others could be financially devastating without it. At CHF 80–150/year, it's the best-value insurance you'll buy.

What does household contents insurance NOT cover? ▾

Structural damage to the building itself (covered by building insurance, usually included in rent), vehicles, high-value jewelry above policy limits, and items you leave unsupervised in public. Always read the exclusions before buying.

My employer has accident insurance — what do I still need? ▾

If you work 8+ hours per week for one employer, they cover workplace accidents (UVG). However, non-workplace (leisure) accidents are covered by your health insurance only if you've added accident coverage — check your health insurance policy. Remove UVG from health insurance once employed to save ~7% on premium.

How does car insurance work in Switzerland? ▾

Third-party liability (Haftpflicht) is legally mandatory before you can register a car. Partial casco (Teilkasko) adds theft and weather damage. Full casco (Vollkasko) also covers your own collision damage. Premiums depend on car value, your canton, claim history, and annual mileage. Compare on Comparis or moneyland.ch.
