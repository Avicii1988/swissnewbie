Premium Subsidy (Prämienverbilligung / IPV)

2027 premiums rose again — but around 30% of people living in Switzerland get help paying them through a cantonal subsidy. Here's how it works and how to check if you qualify.

Who is this for? Anyone with a lower or middle income living in Switzerland — newcomers and long-term residents alike. Eligibility is based on income, not nationality or permit type.

1. What is the premium subsidy?

The premium subsidy — Prämienverbilligung (IPV) in German, réduction de prime in French, riduzione dei premi in Italian — is cantonal financial support toward your mandatory basic health insurance (Grundversicherung/LAMal) premium. It exists because basic health insurance is compulsory for everyone living in Switzerland, regardless of income, and premiums are a flat amount per person rather than scaled to earnings.

It is not a federal program with one national set of rules. Each of the 26 cantons runs its own subsidy scheme, sets its own income thresholds, and pays out a different amount for a comparable situation. This guide explains the parts that are consistent everywhere and flags exactly where you need to check your own canton.

2. Who qualifies

Eligibility is based on taxable household income and household size, assessed by your canton — not on nationality, permit type, or how long you've lived in Switzerland. In practice:

  • Lower and middle-income individuals and families are the main target group
  • Students, part-time workers and single-income families are commonly eligible
  • Income thresholds vary substantially by canton — a household that qualifies in one canton may not in another with the same income
  • Around 30% of people living in Switzerland receive some level of subsidy
⚠️
There is no single income cutoff that applies nationally. Use your canton's official subsidy calculator (usually on the cantonal tax or health department website) to get a real answer for your situation.

3. How much you could get

The amount varies enormously — from a small partial contribution to covering a large share of the premium for very low incomes. It depends on your canton, your taxable income, and your household composition (single, couple, with or without children). There is no single national figure to quote honestly here, so we won't invent one.

What's consistent everywhere: the subsidy is paid toward your premium, regardless of which insurer you choose. It doesn't restrict you to a specific insurer or model — you keep full freedom to compare and pick the cheapest suitable option on priminfo.admin.ch, the same way as anyone not receiving a subsidy.

4. How to apply

The exact process differs by canton, but the general pattern is:

  1. Check your canton's official subsidy page (usually run by the cantonal tax office or a dedicated health insurance subsidy office)
  2. In some cantons, if you're already in the tax system, the subsidy is calculated and paid automatically — no application needed
  3. In others, you need to submit an application form, sometimes together with your tax return
  4. Newcomers without Swiss tax history: most cantons still let you apply, using an estimated or declared income for your first year, since no Swiss tax assessment exists yet. Contact your cantonal office directly to confirm the process
  5. Approved subsidies are typically paid directly to your insurer, reducing your monthly bill — you don't usually receive the money yourself

Search "Prämienverbilligung [your canton]" or "réduction de prime [your canton]" to find your canton's specific page, or ask at your Gemeinde/commune when you register.

5. Timing for the 2027 season

2027 premiums are out and the market average rose +5.0% to around CHF 412/month — the same season when you have your annual window to switch insurer (cancellation must reach your insurer by 30 November 2026). It's worth checking your subsidy eligibility at the same time you're comparing insurers, since:

  • A subsidy you already receive doesn't change if you switch insurer — so there's no reason not to compare and switch for the best premium
  • If your income changed this year (new job, reduced hours, a child), it's worth re-checking eligibility even if you didn't qualify before
  • Application deadlines for the subsidy itself vary by canton and aren't the same as the 30 November insurer-switching deadline — check both separately

6. Frequently asked questions

Does the premium subsidy limit which insurer I can choose? ▾

No. The subsidy is paid toward your mandatory health insurance premium regardless of which insurer or model you choose. You can still compare and switch insurer freely on priminfo.admin.ch — the subsidy follows you to whichever insurer you pick.

Do I need to reapply for the subsidy every year? ▾

It depends on the canton. Some cantons recalculate and renew the subsidy automatically based on your latest tax data; others require you to reapply each year. Check your specific canton's process — this is not standardised nationally.

Can newcomers without Swiss tax history apply for the subsidy? ▾

Yes, in most cantons. Newcomers typically apply with an estimated or declared income for their first year, since no Swiss tax assessment exists yet. The exact process varies by canton — contact your cantonal tax or health insurance subsidy office directly.

How much is the premium subsidy worth? ▾

It varies enormously by canton, household income and household size — from a small partial contribution to covering a large share of the premium for very low incomes. There is no single national figure. Around 30% of Swiss residents receive some level of subsidy. Check your canton's official calculator for a specific estimate.
