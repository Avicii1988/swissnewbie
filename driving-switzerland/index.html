Driving in Switzerland

Driving in Switzerland is straightforward, but the rules are strict and fines are high. As a new resident you have a fixed deadline to exchange your foreign licence, and on the motorway you need a vignette. Here are the rules that matter most.

Who is this for? Expats who drive in Switzerland — with their own car, a rental or a borrowed car.

Exchanging your foreign driving licence

A foreign driving licence is valid in Switzerland for 12 months after you take up residence. Within that period you must exchange it for a Swiss licence at the road traffic office of your canton (Strassenverkehrsamt / service des automobiles). After the deadline, driving on your foreign licence counts as driving without a valid licence.

Typically you need:

  • the application form of your canton
  • your valid foreign licence (original)
  • a passport photo
  • an eye test, done by an optician or doctor
  • a copy of your residence permit or ID

Licences from EU/EFTA countries and a number of other countries are exchanged without a test. Holders of other licences usually have to pass a practical control drive. Fees are set by the canton. Start early — processing can take several weeks.

Motorway vignette

To use Swiss motorways and expressways, cars and motorcycles up to 3.5 tonnes need a vignette. It costs CHF 40 per year and is valid from 1 December of the previous year until 31 January of the following year. Trailers and caravans need their own vignette.

  • e-vignette: since August 2023 the vignette is also available digitally. It is linked to your licence plate and bought online on the federal portal.
  • Sticker: the classic sticker is still sold at post offices, petrol stations and border crossings. It must be stuck to the windscreen.

Driving on the motorway without a valid vignette is fined.

100 km/h

120 km/h

Signposted limits always take priority. Speed cameras are common, and devices or apps that warn you of speed checks are prohibited.

Alcohol limits

  • The general limit is 0.5 per mille blood alcohol.
  • A limit of 0.1 per mille — effectively zero — applies to learner drivers, drivers with a probationary licence and professional drivers.

Driving under the influence leads to fines, licence withdrawal and, in serious cases, criminal proceedings.

Winter driving

There is no general legal obligation to use winter tyres in Switzerland. However, your car must be roadworthy for the conditions: if you cause an accident or block traffic with unsuitable tyres, you can be fined and your insurance may reduce its payment. Snow chains are mandatory where signposted, and you must clear snow and ice from windows, lights and the roof before driving.

Fines and other rules

Minor offences such as slight speeding or parking violations are punished with fixed fines. More serious offences go through criminal proceedings: fines can depend on your income, and your licence can be withdrawn. Very serious speeding can lead to prison sentences.

  • Using a hand-held phone while driving is prohibited.
  • Headlights (dipped beam or daytime running lights) must be on during the day.
  • A warning triangle must be in the car.

Car insurance and borrowed cars

Motor vehicle liability insurance is mandatory before you can register a car; partial and fully comprehensive cover are optional. See our Insurance guide for an overview. If you regularly borrow other people's cars, check whether your private liability insurance covers driving third-party vehicles — more in our guide to borrowed-car cover (Fremdlenkerversicherung) (in German).

Frequently asked questions

How long can I drive with a foreign licence in Switzerland? ▾

For 12 months after you take up residence. Within that period you must exchange it for a Swiss licence at your cantonal road traffic office.

How much does the Swiss motorway vignette cost? ▾

CHF 40 per year. It is valid from 1 December of the previous year until 31 January of the following year and is available as an e-vignette or as a sticker.

What is the speed limit on Swiss motorways? ▾

120 km/h, unless a lower limit is signposted. Expressways have a general limit of 100 km/h.

What is the alcohol limit for driving in Switzerland? ▾

0.5 per mille in general, and 0.1 per mille for learner drivers, probationary licence holders and professional drivers.

Are winter tyres mandatory in Switzerland? ▾

No, there is no general obligation, but your car must be suitable for the conditions. With unsuitable tyres you risk fines and reduced insurance payments.
