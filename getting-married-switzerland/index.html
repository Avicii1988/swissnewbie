Getting Married in Switzerland

In Switzerland only a civil marriage at the civil registry office is legally valid. For expats the paperwork takes the most time: foreign documents, translations and proof of legal residence. Here is the process step by step — and what marriage means for your permit.

Who is this for? Expats planning to marry in Switzerland — with a Swiss partner, another foreigner or someone living abroad.

Legal requirements

  • Both partners must be of age and capable of judgement.
  • Neither partner may already be married or in a registered partnership.
  • Close relatives cannot marry each other.
  • Since July 2022, marriage is open to same-sex couples.
  • Foreign nationals must prove that they are legally resident in Switzerland during the preparation procedure.

Documents you need

The civil registry office tells you exactly which documents apply to you. Typically requested:

  • Valid passport or ID
  • Birth certificate and proof of civil status (e.g. certificate of no impediment)
  • Proof of residence and, for foreign nationals, of legal residence in Switzerland
  • If previously married: proof that the marriage ended (divorce decree or death certificate)

Foreign documents often need to be recent, legalised or apostilled and translated. Obtaining them from abroad can take weeks — start early.

The process at the civil registry office

  1. Preparation procedure: you submit the request and documents to the civil registry office (Zivilstandsamt / office de l’état civil) where one of you lives.
  2. Examination: the office checks that all requirements are met and confirms the result in writing.
  3. Ceremony: the civil wedding then takes place within a limited period at a civil registry office of your choice in Switzerland.
  4. Religious ceremony: optional, and has no legal effect on its own.

Ask your civil registry office about current waiting times for appointments, especially for popular dates.

Fees

The civil registry charges fees for the preparation procedure, the ceremony and the documents, according to the federal fee ordinance. Extra fees apply, for example, for ceremonies outside office hours or for checking foreign documents. Ask your civil registry office for the current fees for your case — and budget separately for translations, legalisations and the celebration itself.

Marriage and your residence permit

  • Married to a Swiss citizen or a C permit holder: the foreign spouse generally has a right to a residence permit through family reunification, as long as the couple lives together.
  • EU/EFTA citizens: family reunification follows the Agreement on the Free Movement of Persons.
  • Married to a B permit holder (non-EU/EFTA): family reunification is possible but depends on conditions such as suitable housing and sufficient financial means.
  • Sham marriages: the civil registry office must refuse a marriage that is only meant to get around immigration law.

Spouses of Swiss citizens can later apply for facilitated naturalisation if they meet the conditions. Contact your cantonal migration office for your situation — see our Permits guide.

What changes after the wedding

  • Names: each partner can keep their name, or the couple can choose one of the names as a common family name.
  • Taxes: married couples are taxed jointly.
  • Marital property: unless you sign a marriage contract, the default regime is participation in acquired property.
  • Admin: update your employer, insurers, bank and, if your permit changes, the migration office.

Frequently asked questions

Can foreigners get married in Switzerland? ▾

Yes. Foreign nationals must prove legal residence in Switzerland during the preparation procedure and provide the required, often legalised and translated, documents.

Is a religious wedding legally valid in Switzerland? ▾

No. Only the civil marriage at the civil registry office is legally valid; a religious ceremony is optional.

How long does it take to get married in Switzerland? ▾

It depends mainly on how quickly you can provide the documents. Obtaining foreign documents can take weeks, so start early and ask the civil registry office about appointment times.

Does marrying a Swiss citizen give me a residence permit? ▾

The foreign spouse of a Swiss citizen generally has a right to a residence permit through family reunification, as long as the couple lives together.

Do we have to take the same surname? ▾

No. Each partner can keep their name, or you can choose one of your names as a common family name.

How much does a civil wedding cost? ▾

The civil registry charges fees for the procedure, ceremony and documents. Ask your civil registry office for the current fees; translations and legalisations cost extra.
